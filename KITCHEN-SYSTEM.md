# 2 · Verified Recipe Data

## Sushi rice — VERIFIED
**Cooking:** 1½ cups dry sushi rice (short-grain) : 1¾ cups water. Rinse until water runs mostly clear (3+ rinses). Boil, lowest heat 15 min lid on, off heat rest 10 min, fluff.

**Seasoning (the "sushi vinegar"):** classic ratio **4 : 2 : 1** (rice vinegar : sugar : salt). Scaled for this batch:
- 4 tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar
- 1½ tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt

**Technique:**
- Vinegar must be **unseasoned**. Label check: unseasoned = 0 calories. Seasoned already contains sugar + salt → double-seasoning.
- Season the rice **warm**, then cool to **room temperature** before rolling. Hot rice tears nori and steams it soggy. Cold rice won't absorb.
- **Fold with a cutting motion** (slice down, lift, turn). Never stir — stirring mashes grains.

## Onigiri — ACTIVE (swap 2026-07-09: nori unavailable, replaces the roll)
*(Technique owner-delegated 2026-07-09 — standard onigiri method, not re-verified against a published source.)*
- **Rice: same cook as sushi rice above.** **Skip the vinegar seasoning entirely** — onigiri rice is plain; the seasoning is a **pinch of salt rubbed onto wet palms** before each ball.
- Shape while the rice is **warm, not hot and not cold.** Warm rice is sticky rice — starch binds as it's pressed and cools into shape. Hot rice burns hands; cold rice crumbles.
- **Method:** wet both hands → pinch of salt across the palms → palmful of rice (~½ cup) → press a dent → a spoonful of filling → cap with a little rice → **press-and-rotate into a triangle, 3–4 turns, firm but not crushing.** Re-wet and re-salt between balls.
- **Fillings:** the mayo-free spicy crab mix (below) is the star. Avocado in small pieces optional. Cucumber sticks become the crunchy side, not a filling.
- **Failure mode:** a crumbling onigiri = rice too cold + pressed too gently. Not the salt, not the shape.
- Batch yields **about 8** from 1½ cups dry rice.

## Rolling — VERIFIED (PARKED — needs nori)
1. **SPREAD** — nori shiny side down, rough side up. Rice over the bottom ⅔, ~¼ inch thick. **Leave the top inch bare.**
2. **FILL** — one modest line across the near edge. Underfill on purpose.
3. **ROLL** — lift the mat, roll away from you, tucking filling with fingertips. Dab water on the bare strip to seal. Squeeze gently.
4. **SLICE** — **wet the knife before every cut.** Halve, then each half into thirds = 6 pieces.

Wet fingers throughout — the entire anti-stick strategy. No mat? Plastic wrap over a folded kitchen towel works.
**Failure mode:** rolls burst at the seam from **overfilling + no bare nori strip**, not from rice temperature or roll direction.

## Fillings
- **Cucumber:** halve lengthwise, scrape seeds out with a spoon (seed core = water = soggy roll), cut to pencil-width sticks.
- **Avocado:** ripe = yields slightly under the thumb. Thin strips (rolls) or small pieces (onigiri).
- **Imitation crab:** **already fully cooked.** Package → roll, no heat, no thaw. **Read the label for egg white.**
- **Spicy crab (mayo-free):** shredded crab + hot sauce + 1 tsp sesame oil. Sesame oil supplies the richness mayo normally would.

**Never raw fish.** Grocery-store salmon is not deep-frozen to parasite-killing spec; home freezers don't reach it. Tuna is excluded by a mercury concern.

## Chicken cutlets — VERIFIED
**Why cutlets:** a breast is a wedge — the thin end is dry before the thick end is safe. Slicing horizontally into two even cutlets makes it cook evenly. A geometry problem, not a cooking problem.
**Method:** hand flat on top, knife **parallel to the board**, slow sawing motion through the middle. 4 breasts → 8 cutlets.
**Cooking:** medium-high, thin oil, **3–4 min per side.** Do not crowd the pan (two batches of four).
**Doneness:** no pink in the center, juices run clear, **165°F**. Times are a guide; doneness is the test.
**Failure mode:** a crowded pan traps released moisture, which must boil off before browning → pale, steamed, gray chicken. Gaps let steam escape.

## Thawing — SAFETY CRITICAL
- **Freezer → fridge** is the default. Slow and safe.
- Still icy? **Sealed bag, submerged in cold water, 20–30 min.** Never warm water. **Never the counter** — the surface enters the bacteria-growth range hours before the middle thaws.
- After a cold-water thaw: **cook the same day. Do not refreeze raw.**
- A date on a frozen item is the **freeze date, not an expiry.** Raw meat stays safe indefinitely while continuously frozen; only quality degrades (~9 months).

## Tex-Mex beans
1 can small red beans (drained, rinsed) + splash of chicken broth + 1 tsp cumin + 1 tsp chili powder + pinch salt. Simmer 6–8 min until thick, **in the unwashed chicken pan.**
**Teach fond:** the browned bits left after the chicken are concentrated flavor. Broth dissolves them into the beans. Washing the pan first throws away the best ingredient.

## Vegetables
Broccoli + peppers, ~1 inch water, simmer, lid on, **4–5 min.** Done = bright green, fork meets slight resistance.

## Roasted chickpeas — VERIFIED
1 can, drained + rinsed. **Rub thoroughly dry on a kitchen towel** (loose skins falling off is a bonus — skins hold moisture). Toss with **1–2 tsp olive oil + salt.** Sheet pan, single layer.
**400°F, 25–30 min, shake the pan halfway.** Done = they rattle.
- **Season AFTER baking** (Tajín, paprika). Salt/wet seasonings pull moisture to the surface during baking.
- **They crisp as they cool.** Never judge them straight out of the oven.
- Store in a jar with the **lid loose.** Airtight traps humidity → soft by morning. Good 4–5 days.

**Failure mode:** soft chickpeas = moisture on both ends (wet going in, airtight jar coming out). Not oven temp. Not seasoning order.

## Overnight oats — VERIFIED
**1 : 1 oats to liquid.** ½ cup old-fashioned rolled oats + ½ cup unsweetened almond/oat milk + pinch salt + cinnamon. Jar, lid, fridge, minimum 4 hours.
- **Old-fashioned rolled oats only.** Quick oats go mushy; steel-cut stay hard.
- Adding chia seeds? Increase liquid (½ cup oats : ⅔ cup milk).
- Keeps 4–5 days. Layer toppings in the morning, don't pre-mix fruit.
- **This replaces snack slot 1. It does not stack on top of it.**

## Rice (the non-sushi kind)
1 cup extra long grain : 1¾ cups water, pinch salt. Boil → lowest heat 15 min lid on → off, rest 5 → fluff.
**Basmati vs jasmine:** basmati has a notably **lower glycemic index**; jasmine is one of the higher-GI white rices. For a weight-loss goal, **basmati wins — do not switch to jasmine.** The real upgrade is brown basmati (fiber).

## Storage & reheating
- Cooked chicken/beef: **3–4 days.** Cooked salmon: **~3 days.** Cooked rice: **3–4 days.**
- Cool ~20 min until just warm, **then** lid and fridge. Lidding hot food traps steam and warms the whole fridge.
- Cooked rice into the fridge within ~1 hour (it can grow a bacterium that survives reheating).
- Reheat until **steaming hot all the way through**, not merely warm. Reheat a portion once, never twice.
- Don't leave cooked food out more than ~2 hours total.
